Welcome to TechResQ

Technology makes studying easier, but computer emergencies can interrupt schoolwork and put personal information at risk.

Use these modules to learn how to prevent common problems and make informed decisions when something goes wrong.

Remember: If you suspect a security incident, avoid entering passwords on a suspicious device. Ask a trusted IT administrator or technician for help when needed.

Preventive Modules

🔒

1. Cybersecurity

Use strong, unique passwords and enable multifactor authentication. Keep your operating system and security software updated.

Avoid suspicious links, unexpected attachments, and requests for passwords or verification codes.

💾

2. Data Protection

Back up important school files to a trusted external drive or cloud service. Check that your backups can actually be restored.

Keep multiple copies when possible and protect access to your files.

🔌

3. Power and Hardware

Use a suitable surge protector or UPS when appropriate. Keep vents clear, avoid liquids near devices, and inspect cables for damage.

Shut down equipment properly and never open power supplies yourself.

🌐

4. Safe Internet Use

Download software from trusted sources. Check website addresses, use secure networks, and avoid sharing sensitive information on public computers.

Emergency Response Guides

🦠 Malware or Suspicious Activity

1. Stop interacting with suspicious pop-ups and links.

2. If you suspect an active infection, disconnect the device from Wi-Fi or the network if you can do so safely.

3. Use trusted security tools or contact IT support. Do not download random "cleaner" programs or enter credentials into suspicious prompts.

4. From a trusted, clean device, change affected passwords and notify the relevant school or service administrator if accounts may be compromised.

📁 Lost or Deleted Files

1. Stop saving or installing things on the affected drive to reduce the risk of overwriting recoverable data.

2. Check the Recycle Bin or Trash, file history, and trusted backups.

3. Restore files from a known-good backup if available.

4. If the data is valuable and the drive may be failing, stop using it and consult a qualified recovery professional. Avoid writing recovery tools to the affected drive.

⚡ Overheating, Power Failure, or Hardware Trouble

1. If you smell burning, see sparks, or notice a swollen battery, stop using the device and move away from danger. Do not charge it or handle a damaged battery.

2. For smoke or fire, evacuate and contact emergency services. Do not open a power supply or attempt electrical repairs.

3. If there is no immediate hazard, shut down normally when possible, check external connections, and let an overheating device cool.

4. Contact a qualified technician if the problem continues.
